This document does not select vendors, does not record prices and is not a security or compliance certification.

The handoff requiring this matrix is [DEEPSEEK-HANDOFF](DEEPSEEK-HANDOFF.md), item 6.

## 1. How to read this document

- Row IDs are gate IDs. Each matrix row has exactly one entry in the gate register in section 7, and the count of rows equals the count of gates. There are no closed gates.
- Evidence labels follow [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md): live-observed, historical-reference, vendor-claimed, inferred, proposed. Every row here is proposed unless it cites an observation.
- Gate status vocabulary:
  - **blocked-on-discovery** - cannot close without a contract, a written confirmation or a received sample from a party outside this project.
  - **open** - ours to close; no external party is required, but no approved decision record exists yet.
  - **decided** - closed with cited written evidence committed to this repository.
- **Cost model.** No price, quote, per-transaction fee or subscription figure has been obtained for any class. "unknown - requires vendor contact" states the absence of commercial data; it is not an invitation to estimate.
- **Sandbox.** No partner sandbox is known to exist or to be available to this project. Where a row says a synthetic stand-in is planned, that is our own fake adapter as described in the plans; it is not built, and it proves nothing about a vendor.
- **Pilot necessity.** *Required* means the declared replacement release cannot be claimed without it. *Optional* means a documented fallback keeps the release credible. Where a row is conditional, the condition is stated.
- **Accountable owner** is a role, never a person. A person may hold several roles; the responsibility remains distinct for each. Named-person assignment for each role is a prerequisite tracked outside this file.

### Standing constraints every row inherits

- No PHI in development, fixtures, issues, logs, demos or trial accounts. A free tier is not authorization, per [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) and [SECURITY-AND-READINESS](SECURITY-AND-READINESS.md).
- No licensed terminology dataset in the repository. Synthetic placeholder codes in fixtures until rights are resolved.
- No private endpoint scraping of any incumbent system. Practice-authorized exports or documented APIs only, with rights verified.
- Every integration sits behind a narrow adapter port, fails visibly with an explicit unknown state, and has a staff-visible fallback path.
- Core function, export and self-hosting must not depend on a proprietary feature key or an add-on tier.
- No clinical, financial or adjudication claim may be inferred from a menu label, a selector value or a public marketing page.

## 4. What cannot be assumed

Each item below has been, or plausibly could be, treated as settled. None is settled. Until a contract, a written confirmation or a received sample exists, none may appear in a plan, a cost estimate, a demo or a sales conversation as fact.

1. **That any vendor will contract with this project at all**, at any price, in any timeline, or under terms compatible with open-source distribution and self-hosting.
2. **That the incumbent will provide a complete export**, that an export fee or format is knowable, or that the practice's agreement permits using the output to populate another system.
3. **That an advertised API means usable API access.** A public page advertising REST APIs and an MCP service is a vendor claim. It does not establish documented rights, rate limits, a version-stable contract or access under the practice's agreement.
4. **That any sandbox exists.** No partner has confirmed a test environment, test credentials, test payers or synthetic claim data.
5. **That an observed menu label, selector value or endpoint name proves capability.** Imaging Studio, payment gateway configuration, a lab section, referral overlays and a prescribing-account endpoint are labels or paths. None proves a workflow, an integration, an enforcement rule or an available interface.
6. **That the observed EPCS verification identity means this practice, or we, can prescribe controlled substances.** The label was seen in a user list. Its authority, its permissions, the identity-proofing behind it and whether it is active for this practice are unconfirmed.
7. **That industry-standard transaction formats are supported by any chosen partner.** Dental claim and remittance formats and prescribing transaction standards are conventions; whether a specific partner supports a specific version, and under what guide, requires the partner's document.
8. **That a BAA will be available, or that a BAA alone authorizes a design.** Availability, scope, subprocessor flow-down and region are all contract facts.
9. **That a free tier, trial credit or "HIPAA-ready" marketing is authorization for PHI.** It is not. It is not authorization in this repository either: the development project stays synthetic.
10. **That a card token can be used indefinitely, or that expiry and reissue behavior is known.** Token type, scope, lifetime and update capability are processor facts.
11. **That we can schedule auto-pay.** Whether the schedule is ours or the processor's determines the entire failure model, and it is not established.
12. **That a lab or referral partner accepts electronic orders or returns.** Neither channel was observed, and print is the incumbent's own visible artifact.
13. **That a CDT or other licensed dataset may be redistributed** with open-source software, in a self-hosted install, or to a practice that does not hold its own license.
14. **That DICOM support implies a conformance statement, or that a candidate parser's license is compatible** with our distribution.
15. **That the practice's existing SMS or email consents transfer to a new sender**, a new number or a new channel.
16. **That eligibility data may be retained and reused freely**, or that an eligibility response is authoritative for coverage or patient responsibility.
17. **That a vendor outage has a workable staff fallback**, or that the fallback was ever exercised.
18. **That migration completeness can be verified from the incumbent's own counts.** A manifest can understate what the export contains; only artifact comparison detects it.

## 5. Data-processing and PHI boundaries

This section states where PHI would flow and what has to exist before it may. It is a design boundary, not a legal opinion. Legal characterizations (business associate, conduit, de-identified) require a written determination by counsel and cannot be established by engineering.

### 5.1 Which classes would receive PHI

| Class | PHI it would receive | Minimum-necessary question to settle | Business-associate position | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clearinghouse | Yes: identity, coverage, procedure codes, fees, dates, narratives, attachments | Is narrative text or a chart excerpt required, or does a code suffice for this payer? | Expected business associate; confirm in the contract | Claim data is a payment disclosure. The trading-partner agreement governs transaction format; privacy terms come from the BAA. |
| Eligibility | Yes: identity, coverage, and sometimes service context | Must procedure or diagnosis codes be sent to obtain benefits at all? | Expected | Retention terms limit how long a response may be kept and reused. |
| E-prescribing and EPCS | Yes: patient, prescriber, medication, pharmacy | Does medication history require a separate consent or disclosure basis? | Expected; EPCS adds identity-proofing records | Proofing and credential records are not clinical content but are sensitive and auditable. |
| Payments | Yes, in the sense of patient identity with amounts and sometimes procedure text; card data is cardholder data, not automatically PHI | Does a statement descriptor or memo field reveal treatment? | Expected: the processor receives patient identity and a financial record | Tokenization reduces PCI scope. It does not reduce HIPAA obligations. |
| Messaging (SMS, email, telephony) | Yes, if content names a provider, an appointment type or a balance; a voicemail recording is PHI | Can the message carry no clinical content and point to the portal? | Expected for SMS and email vendors; telephony if voicemail or recordings are stored | Provider content retention is a disclosure in itself and must be contracted. |

| Migration and archive | Yes: the entire record, in bulk | Who may receive the package, and where are intermediate copies destroyed? | Importing a vendor export does not create a BAA with the incumbent; the practice authorizes the disclosure | Highest-risk class: bulk files, transfer channel, staging retention, and de-identification for any sample we hold. |

### 5.2 What a business associate agreement implies

A BAA permits a business associate to use or disclose PHI for specified purposes, requires appropriate safeguards, flows the obligations to subcontractors, sets breach notification duties, and requires return or destruction of data at termination. From that it follows that:

- A BAA is a permission with a scope. A vendor may hold a BAA and still be unusable for a given integration because its scope excludes that service, that region or that subprocessor.
- A BAA does not make an architecture secure, does not transfer our own Security Rule obligations to the vendor, and does not substitute for a risk analysis, access control testing or an incident procedure.
- A vendor that will not sign a BAA cannot receive PHI from us, however useful the product is. That is a selection constraint, not a negotiation tactic.
- Subprocessors inherit the obligation. A vendor's own list of subcontractors, and its flow-down terms, are part of the review.
- Termination terms matter as much as use terms: data must be returnable and deletable, and the deletion must be evidenced.

### 5.3 A free development tier is not authorization for PHI

The development project, any trial account, any sandbox and any "HIPAA-ready" plan are not authorization to process PHI. They are authorization to process synthetic data. This repository already treats that as a hard boundary ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), [SECURITY-AND-READINESS](SECURITY-AND-READINESS.md)): no real records, no production exports, no patient charts, no licensed datasets. Two consequences apply to every row in this matrix:

- An integration may be demonstrated end to end against synthetic data and a fake adapter without any BAA. That demonstration does not authorize the same integration with a real patient, and must never be described as validated.
- A trial account created to evaluate an interface is a synthetic-data environment until a BAA, a security review and an organizational PHI authorization exist for it. Availability is not permission.

### 5.4 Conduit and de-identification are legal positions, not engineering defaults

Do not label a messaging, hosting or network vendor a mere conduit, and do not label a dataset de-identified, without a written determination. Both labels change obligations, and both have been applied loosely in this industry. Our default is to assume the stricter position.

### 5.5 Places PHI leaks without anyone deciding to disclose it

URLs, object paths, file names, log fields, error text, message bodies, notification previews, calendar and email subjects, support screenshots, browser history and test fixtures. Each adapter needs a stated redaction rule and a test that the rule is enforced. The observed incumbent convention of carrying a session token in the URL path is exactly the pattern our own API must not repeat ([API surface](REFERENCE-API-OBSERVATIONS.md)).

### 5.6 Vendor support access

Remote support, troubleshooting and "send us the record so we can reproduce it" are PHI disclosures. The contract must state how support access is granted, logged, time-bounded and terminated, and the practice must be able to refuse it. Support that requires a live patient record is a contract term, not an operational detail.

### Gate closure rules

- A gate closes on written evidence committed to the repository, or on an approved decision record with a named closing role and a date. It does not close on a sales call, a marketing page, a verbal assurance or an internal assumption.
- A gate may be recorded as *decided with a fallback* when the fallback is what ships. It is then named in the release record, and the capability it replaces must be described as unavailable rather than as planned.
- A gate status may move backwards. If a contract term changes or a sample contradicts a prior closure, the gate reopens and the prior closure is retained with a superseding entry rather than deleted.
- No gate may be closed on the accountable owner's assertion alone where a second discipline is required: security gates require the Security & Compliance Owner, clinical gates require the Clinical Lead, and financial gates require the Billing Operations Lead, irrespective of who prepared the evidence.

## 9. Maintenance and change control

- This file is updated only by its owner. Each change names the gate IDs affected, the evidence added and the status change.
- New integration classes are added as new sections with a new gate prefix, not by renumbering existing gates; a retired gate keeps its ID and is marked withdrawn with a reason.
- A gate that cannot be closed for a pilot is recorded in the release record as an unavailable capability with its fallback. It is not deleted from this matrix.
- The matrix is a planning artifact. It becomes a procurement and onboarding checklist only when the Product Owner assigns named holders to the roles in section 8 and a pilot practice is identified.
- Any row whose cost cell still reads "unknown - requires vendor contact" cannot appear in a practice-facing proposal or budget.
