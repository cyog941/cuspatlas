# Readiness review: decision log, risk and unknown register, verdict

Date: 2026-11-03. Status: review of a planning record only. Nothing in the product is implemented.

## Scope and method

This review reads the repository as it stands and separates four things the documents often blur: what has been decided, what is still open, what could go wrong, and what is not known. It adds no new requirements and resolves no open decision. Where a plan declared an open question, it is carried here with the plan's own recommended option and the task that option blocks.

Documents read: [working rules](../AGENTS.md), [handoff](DEEPSEEK-HANDOFF.md), [roadmap](ROADMAP.md), [security and readiness](SECURITY-AND-READINESS.md), [analysis coverage](TAB32-ANALYSIS-COVERAGE.md), [research ledger](RESEARCH.md), [design](superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-dental-pms-design.md), the seven plans present in [plans](superpowers/plans/), [shared contracts](SHARED-CONTRACTS.md), [capabilities](CAPABILITIES.md), [API and MCP](API-AND-MCP.md), [foundation status](SETUP.md), [design review](REVIEW.md), and the four Tab32 companion documents ([surface map](TAB32-AUTHENTICATED-MAP.md), [HTTP surface](TAB32-API-SURFACE.md), [domain model](TAB32-DOMAIN-MODEL.md), [state vocabularies](TAB32-STATUS-VOCABULARIES.md)).

Limits of this review: no git history, no network, no live product, and no MCP analysis tool was used. Provisioning and issue state are reported as recorded in [SETUP.md](SETUP.md), not reverified. Every claim below about Tab32 is a claim about what this repository recorded observing, not a claim established here. Several of these documents were still being revised while this review was written, so a citation may point at a slightly older revision than the one now on disk; a specific number or link should be rechecked against the current file before it is relied on.

## Part 3. Readiness verdict

| Question | Answer | One-sentence justification |
|---|---|---|
| Is the research phase complete? | **No** | The coverage ledger states its own status as incomplete: chart and periodontal surfaces are essentially untested, no transaction was executed in any module, no failing request was made, and no role enforcement, payer, device, or export qualification exists. |
| Is the specification package complete? | **No** | Architecture, tenancy, audit, API/MCP, security, capability, and migration design documents exist, but there is no consolidated role matrix, no standalone synthetic acceptance scenario pack, no integration/device/migration qualification matrix with named owners, and forty-nine open decisions remain. |
| Is the implementation plan set complete? | **No** | Seven dependency-ordered plans exist for the platform core and six module workstreams, but the roadmap states that an executable plan exists for every workstream and links plan files for workstreams A (API/MCP and AI) and R (supported release) that are absent from the repository, prescribing has no plan, and twenty-one of the open decisions are not task-mapped. |
| Is any part of this product built? | **No** | The repository contains planning, policy, and setup documentation only: no application code, no framework dependencies, no SQL migrations, no clinical schema, no deployment, no API service, and no UI. |
| Is it safe to use with real patient data? | **No** | There is no schema, no tested isolation, no BAA, no risk assessment, no penetration test, and no restore drill, and the development project is explicitly synthetic-data only. |
| Is it clinically validated? | **No** | No clinician has validated chart semantics, periodontal calculations, note and signature rules, consent capture, or alert handling in this phase; the recorded review was an architecture review, not clinical sign-off. |
| Is it production ready? | **No** | None of the ten production gates in the security document is evidenced, and the self-hosting claim itself is explicitly not yet supported. |

**Planning progress is not readiness.** A complete-looking document set means the questions have been written down; it does not mean the product is production ready, clinically validated, or that the incumbent has been fully reverse engineered. Those are three separate claims, each requiring evidence this repository does not have.

What the repository does support: a defensible scope boundary, a labeled evidence ledger with real authenticated captures, a coherent separation of clinical and financial facts, a security posture stated as gates rather than certifications, and seven plans whose blocked items are honestly marked. That is a legitimate planning asset. It is not a release.

## Part 4. What the next human action must be

Ordered by what unblocks the most work. Each item names a role, not a person.

1. **Ratify or reject the provisional technical direction** — stack, web application with optional companion, tenancy and deployment model, and the first-replacement-release contract. Why it blocks: every plan and every acceptance case presupposes them, and they are currently labeled recommendations, not approvals. Unblocked by: product owner with the engineering lead.
2. **Commit to one pilot practice and name its reviewers** — clinical, billing, reception, and practice-management decision authority. Why it blocks: most of the forty-nine open decisions and every vendor qualification depend on a real practice's inventory, workflow, and sign-off; without it, discovery has no denominator. Unblocked by: product owner and the practice owner.
3. **Obtain a written export authorization and one sample package from the incumbent** — or a written refusal. Why it blocks: migration, the exit-path promise, and the cutover gate all rest on it, and a refusal changes the product's central claim. Unblocked by: practice owner, with legal counsel.
4. **Secure contracts and sandboxes for clearinghouse, payment processor, eligibility, messaging, telephony, and prescribing** — or declare a limited trial explicitly. Why it blocks: claims, ERA, reminders, and e-prescribing are declared first-release capabilities; without partners, the release cannot be called replacement-ready. Unblocked by: operations lead, with legal counsel for BAA and data-processing terms.
5. **Engage the clinical reviewer to validate chart, periodontal, note, consent, and alert semantics** — with written sign-off per capability row. Why it blocks: clinical tasks 6–13 and OPEN-09 through OPEN-18 cannot be closed on inference, and unvalidated clinical semantics is the highest-consequence defect class. Unblocked by: clinical lead.
6. **Engage the billing reviewer to settle money policy** — rounding, adjustments, undistributed funds, statement delivery, and discrepancy tolerance. Why it blocks: ledger and allocation tasks would otherwise encode an unratified accounting rule. Unblocked by: billing reviewer.
7. **Commission legal review** — terminology and code-set distribution rights, retention by jurisdiction and legal hold, guardianship authority, cross-practice disclosure, and license governance. Why it blocks: bundling or omitting a code set, or shipping one universal retention period, is a defect that cannot be fixed by engineering later. Unblocked by: legal counsel.
8. **Name the security owner and commission the risk assessment** — including audit retention and tamper-resistance approach. Why it blocks: the production gate requires a named accountable owner before any other security evidence counts. Unblocked by: product owner and privacy officer.
9. **Name the hosting operator and agree the recovery objective** — hosting model, RPO and RTO, and the escalation path. Why it blocks: self-hosting cannot move from design objective to supported capability without an owner and a measured drill. Unblocked by: operator and product owner.
