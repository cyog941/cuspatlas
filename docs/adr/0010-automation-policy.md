# ADR 0010: Automation policy for proposals and approvals (synthetic foundation)

Status: accepted for the synthetic foundation, 2026-11-04 (F Task 7a). Evidence category: configured and tested on synthetic data on one Windows host (tests/integration/proposal-policy.test.ts). Not evidence for any clinical, financial, messaging or access-administration operation; each of those needs its own operation-specific policy and review before it may use this mechanism.

## Context

The AI architecture contract requires that a change recommended by automation goes through read, recommend, draft, approve and execute; that a server-created proposal binds tenant, requesting principal, exact operation and payload digest, affected record versions, expiry and policy version; that editing the payload invalidates approval; that execution rechecks access and versions; and that an agent cannot approve its own elevated action. The frozen contract decisions ("Worker and proposals") fix the states, the human-only approver and the use of `executeOnce` with the proposal id as idempotency key. No live model or provider exists in this foundation; proposals are prepared by synthetic service principals and humans in tests.

## Decision

Scope: the only operations a proposal may carry are `syntheticTasks.create` and `syntheticTasks.relabel`.

| Step | Who may do it | Bound or checked |
|---|---|---|
| prepare (`syntheticTasks.proposals.prepare`) | human or service with `syntheticTasks.read` (an agent may draft what it could never execute alone); location rules apply | server-generated id; tenant and proposer from the authenticated context; operation; stored payload and its canonical SHA-256 digest; task version (`expectedVersion` must equal the current one); `PROPOSAL_POLICY_VERSION`; expiry now + 15 min (max 1 day). No task change, no audit/outbox row, a `prepared` provenance row. Idempotency-Key required |
| read (`syntheticTasks.proposals.read`) | `syntheticTasks.read` AND (proposer, approver, committer, a human who could approve, or a member with write); location rules apply | otherwise 404 identical to missing and to other tenants' proposals. Read-only; an expired-but-unmarked proposal is reported as `expired` without writing |
| approve (`syntheticTasks.proposals.approve`) | human with `syntheticTasks.approve` (and location access); never the proposer when the proposer is a service principal (`self_approval_denied`, checked before scope so the reason is explicit) | the approver submits the digest it reviewed (mismatch → `payload_mismatch`, no state change); policy version must equal the server's (`policy_changed`, proposal rejected); not expired (`proposal_expired`, proposal expired). Approval record (approver, kind, time, policy version, digest, execution mode) set once, by trigger-checked acting principal. Idempotency key = proposal id |
| commit (`syntheticTasks.proposals.commit`) | executor authorized for the proposal's own operation (`syntheticTasks.write`), live; location of the task rechecked | submitted payload digest = bound digest (else rejected `payload_mismatch`); stored payload digest, approval digest and approval policy version consistent (else rejected `approval_invalid`); current policy version (else rejected `policy_changed`); approver still a live human with approve scope (else rejected, 403); proposer still a live member with prepare rights (else rejected, 403); not expired; task version unchanged (else rejected `stale_version`). Then the mutation, audit and outbox through `executeOnce` (key = proposal id), and the proposal is `committed` with its result in the same transaction |
| deferred execution | approval with `execution: deferred` writes one outbox event on the `actionProposal` aggregate (plus its audit twin); the worker leases it, reauthorizes the approver (event actor) live, and executes as its own service principal through the same commit path (no submitted payload; stored payload digest must still match) | the request never reaches an adapter; refusals dead-letter with `membership_revoked`, `authorization_denied` or `proposal_refused` |

Human proposer approving its own proposal: allowed. The frozen rule forbids same-principal approval only when the proposer is a service principal, and this ADR keeps it that way for the synthetic operations. Separation of duties between humans (for example a clinician who drafts and a second clinician who signs, or a refund requested and approved by different staff) is an operation-specific policy decision and must be stated by each future policy; nothing here implies it is satisfied.

States and closure: `prepared → approved → committed`, `prepared|approved → expired|rejected`. A refusal that makes the proposal permanently unusable closes it and is committed before the refusal is returned (the commit-then-refuse path); later calls replay the same refusal code. A refused executor (revoked, lacking scope, location) does not close it, because another authorized executor may still commit. A committed proposal returns its recorded result with `replayed: true` to any authorized caller forever; the proposal row (locked `FOR UPDATE`) is the durable once-only guard and the `executeOnce` reservation (7-day retention) is the second guard.

Refusal codes added to the shared contract: `self_approval_denied` (403), `payload_mismatch`, `proposal_expired`, `policy_changed`, `approval_invalid` (409). Every refusal of a proposal operation, not only denials, writes a security attempt row with an internal reason.

Storage: `action_proposals` (RLS enabled and forced, own tenant; the runtime role may INSERT for itself as proposer with its real kind and UPDATE only state, version, approval, commit-result and disposition columns; a trigger enforces the state machine, immutable binding, approval-once by the acting principal over the bound digest and policy version, and commit-by-acting-principal; a CHECK forbids service self-approval; no DELETE). `action_proposal_provenance` is append-only (trigger), tenant-scoped, and holds step, actor, kind, policy version, digest, closing reason and result reference only, never payload text. Ordinary logs carry correlation ids and codes only.

Policy version: `PROPOSAL_POLICY_VERSION` in `packages/domain/src/policy/authorize.ts` (`synthetic-proposals.1`). Bump it whenever a rule an approver relied on changes; outstanding approvals then fail closed (`policy_changed`).

## Consequences and limits

- Proposal content is protected application data. Readers who may approve or execute see the payload; nobody else learns that a proposal exists.
- The proposer's and approver's memberships are loaded immediately before the commit transaction (a pool connection cannot be used inside it); a revocation that lands in that window is not seen by that one commit. The executor's own membership is rechecked inside the transaction.
- A privileged role (owner or superuser) can bypass the trigger; the commit-time rechecks of digest, approval record and policy version are what make such tampering fail closed. Tamper evidence against privileged roles remains a deployment gate (ADR 0002).
- Restore (Task 8) must preserve proposal state, approval columns, digests, policy versions, expiry and provenance. A restored `prepared`/`approved` proposal whose expiry passed fails closed (`proposal_expired`); a restored approved proposal whose task version moved fails `stale_version`; a restored committed proposal replays its result and never re-executes, even when the `executeOnce` reservation is gone.
- Future operation-specific policies must state: which operations require a proposal (direct write is still available to write-scoped principals in this synthetic domain), who may propose, approve and execute, human separation of duties, maximum lifetime, batch semantics with per-item outcomes, consent/opt-out rechecks, and model/provider/prompt provenance once a live model exists.
- Out of scope: live models, clinical signing, financial posting, messaging, exports, access administration.

## Hardening after independent review of 6540a56 (2026-11-04)

- Approve rechecks the live task version (non-locking read inside the approval transaction): a relabel proposal whose task already moved is closed `rejected` with reason `stale_version`, provenance step `rejected`, and the caller gets 409 `stale_version`. The commit-time locked recheck is unchanged and stays the authoritative guard for changes between approval and commit.
- Database defense in depth (migration `20261104060000_proposal_approver_human.sql`): the guard trigger also requires the approver row to be a `human` principal, independent of the caller-written `approver_kind`.
- MCP: the propose/approve/commit tools validate in the domain (`onInvalid: 'domain'`), so their handler runs before the MCP schema verdict. Their input schemas are asserted to equal the domain validator's sub-schemas plus transport properties (`apps/mcp/src/tool-drift.test.ts`), so the MCP schema can never be stricter than the domain.
- Known griefing vector, accepted by design (frozen rule): any principal allowed to execute the proposal's operation (here any write-scoped member) who submits a commit with a payload that differs from the bound one permanently closes an approved proposal as `rejected`/`payload_mismatch`; a new proposal must then be prepared and approved. This fails safe (nothing executes) but lets a write-scoped insider deny service. Future mitigation option, not implemented: an operation policy may require the committer to be the proposer or the approver (committer in {proposer, approver}), or may refuse a mismatched payload without closing the proposal when the committer is neither. Choosing between them belongs to the operation-specific policies listed above.
